ACT ONE

FIRST SCENE

(The Church of Sant' Andrea della Valle. To the right, the Attavanti Chapel. To the left, a painter's scaffold with a large painting covered with cloth. Painter's tools. A basket.)

<img:https://thumb.wikimedia.org/wikipedia/commons/thumb/0/01/Sant%27Andrea_della_Valle_%28Rome%29_-_Interno.jpg/1280px-Sant%27Andrea_della_Valle_%28Rome%29_-_Interno.jpg?utm_source=it.wikipedia.org&amp;utm_campaign=imageinfo&amp;utm_content=thumbnail|Sant'Andrea della Valle (Rome)

ANGELOTTI
(dressed as a prisoner — ragged, disheveled, and trembling with fear — he enters, panting and almost running. He casts a quick glance around.)
Ah! At last! 
In my foolish terror I thought
I saw the mug of a policeman in every face.
(stops to look around attentively with more calm to recognise the place. Sighs with relief as he notices the column with its basin of Holy Water an the Madonna.)
The basin... and the column...
"At the feet of the Madonna"
My sister wrote me...
(Goes up to the Madonna and searches about at the base. Gives a muffled shout of joy as he picks up the key.)
Here is the key!... and here is the chapel!
(He points to the Attavanti Chapel, feverishly inserts the key into the lock, opens the gate, enters the Chapel, closes it again... and disappears.)

SECOND SCENE

SACRISTAN
(appears from the background: moving from right to left, attending to the management of the Church; he holds a bundle of brushes in its hand.)
Forever washing!... And every brush is filthier
Than an underpriest's collar.
Mister Painter... There!...
(looks toward the platform where the painting stands, and seeing it deserted, exclaims in surprise:)
No one! - I would have sworn
That Cavalier Cavaradossi
Had come back.
(puts down the brushes, climbs on the scaffold and examines the basket, remarking:)
No. I'm mistaken. - The basket is untouched.
(climbs down from the scaffolding. The Angelus bell rings. The sacristan kneels and prays in a low voice:)
Angelus Domini nuntiavit Mariae,
Et concepit de Spiritu Sancto.
Ecce ancilla Domini;
Fiat mihi secundum Verbum tuum
Et Verbum caro factum est
[[Et habitavit in nobis...|The Angel of the Lord declared unto Mary, And she conceived of the Holy Spirit. Behold the handmaid of the Lord, Be it done unto me according to thy word. And the Word was made flesh, And dwelt among us...]]

THIRD SCENE

CAVARADOSSI
(from the side door, seeing the Sacristan on his knees)
What are you doing?

SACRISTAN
(rising)
Reciting the Angelus.
(Cavaradossi mounts the scaffold and uncovers the painting: it is of a Mary Magdalene with great blue eyes and a cascade of golden hair. The painter stands in silence before it and studies it closely. The Sacristan turns to speak to Cavaradossi and cries out in amazement as he sees the uncovered picture.)
Oh holy cruets! Her picture!

CAVARADOSSI
(turning to the Sacristan)
Whose?

SACRISTAN
That strange girl 
Who came here to pray in the past days...
(gesturing with an unctuous air toward the Madonna, from whom Angelotti took the key)
All devout - and pious.

CAVARADOSSI
(smiling)
It's true. And she was so absorbed
In her fervent prayer 
That I, unseen, painted her lovely face.

SACRISTAN
(scandalised)
[[Out, Satan, Out!|With this comedic reaction, the Sacristan reveals his weakness.]]

CAVARADOSSI
(to the Sacristan)
Give me my paints.
(The Sacristan obeys. Cavaradossi paints rapidly, with frequent pauses to observe his work. The Sacristan comes and goes; he carries a small basin in which he continues washing the brushes. Suddenly Cavaradossi leaves his painting: from his pocket he takes a medallion with a miniature portrait, and his eyes go from the miniature to his own painting.)
[[Hidden harmony|One of the main arias of act one begins]]
Of diverse beauties! 
Floria is dark-haired, 
my ardent lover...

SACRISTAN
(in an undertone, as if grumbling)
Jest with knaves and leave the saints...
(moves away to get water to clean the brushes)

CAVARADOSSI
And you, unknown beauty…
Crowned with blond locks!
Your eyes are blue,
but Tosca's are dark!

SACRISTAN
(returning from the back, still scandalized:)
Jest with knaves and leave the saints...
(resumes washing the brushes)

CAVARADOSSI
Art in its mystery
confounds together diverse beauties;
but in painting her
my only thought is you, Tosca!
(continues painting)

SACRISTAN
These various women
that compete with the Madonna
reek of Hell.
(dries the washed brushes, all the while muttering)
Jest with knaves and leave the saints...
And as for those dogs of [[Voltairians|Referring to Voltaire, liberal Franch philosopher and critic of the church. The political background of the opera is introduced.]]
enemies of the most Holy Government
one must not even speak of them!...
(he places the basin under the scaffolding and the brushes in a vase, near the painter)
Jest with knaves and leave the saints...
(gesturing towards Cavaradossi)
Indeed they are unrepentant, the whole lot!
Let us rather make the sign of the cross.
(to Cavaradossi)
Excellency, may I go?

CAVARADOSSI
As you wish.
(resumes his painting)

SACRISTAN
(pointing to the basket)
The basket's full...
Are you fasting?

CAVARADOSSI
I'm not hungry.

SACRISTAN
(ironically, rubbing his hands)
Oh! How regretful!
(but he cannot suppress a gesture of joy and a look of greed directed at the basket, which he picks up and sets slightly aside; he sniffs two pinches of tobacco)
Be sure to close up when you leave.

CAVARADOSSI
(painting)
Go!...

SACRISTAN
I'm going.
(He moves away towards the back. Cavaradossi, turning his back to the chapel, works. Angelotti, believing the church to be empty, appears behind the gate and inserts the key to open it.)

FOURTH SCENE

CAVARADOSSI
(turns at the creaking of the lock)
Someone in there!
(At Cavaradossi’s movement, a terrified Angelotti stops short, as if to take refuge back in the chapel — but — as he looks up, a cry of joy, which he instantly stifles in fear, bursts from his chest. He has recognized the painter and stretches out his arms to him, as if to an unexpected savior.)

ANGELOTTI
You! Cavaradossi!
Heaven sent you!
(Cavaradossi does not recognize Angelotti and stands dumbfounded on the scaffolding; Angelotti draws closer so as to be recognized.)
Don't you recognise me?
(with sadness)
Prison has truly changed me then!

CAVARADOSSI
(recognizing him, he quickly sets down his palette and brushes and climbs down from the scaffolding toward Angelotti, glancing cautiously around)
Angelotti! The Consul
Of the lamented Roman Republic!
(runs to close door on the right)

ANGELOTTI
(approaching Cavaradossi mysteriously)
I have just escaped from Castel Sant'Angelo!

CAVARADOSSI
(generously)
I am at your disposal.

TOSCA'S VOICE
Mario!
(at the sound of Tosca's voice, Cavaradossi makes a quick gesture to Angelotti to be silent.)

CAVARADOSSI
Hide! 
She's a... jealous woman!
One moment and I'll send her away.

TOSCA'S VOICE
Mario!

CAVARADOSSI
(towards the door from which Tosca's voice comes)
Here I am!

ANGELOTTI
(overcome by a fit of weakness, he leans against the scaffolding and says painfully:)
I'm faint with exhaustion, 
I can't bear any more...

CAVARADOSSI
(moving very quickly, he climbs onto the platform, comes down with the basket, and hands it to Angelotti)
There's food and wine in this basket!

ANGELOTTI
Thank you!

CAVARADOSSI
(encouraging Angelotti, he pushes him toward the chapel)
Quick!
(Angelotti enters the chapel.)

FIFTH SCENE

TOSCA'S VOICE
(calling out repeatedly, in an irritated tone)
Mario! 

CAVARADOSSI
(feigning calm, he opens the door to Tosca)
I am here!

TOSCA
(enters somewhat violently, brusquely pushes away Mario—who wants to hug her—and looks around suspiciously)
Why was it locked?

CAVARADOSSI
(with feigned indifference)
The Sacristan's demands it...

TOSCA
Who were you talking to?

CAVARADOSSI
To you!

TOSCA
You were whispering other words. Where is she?...

CAVARADOSSI
Who?

TOSCA
Her!... That woman!...
I heard the quick steps
And the rustling of a dress.

CAVARADOSSI
You're dreaming!

TOSCA
Do you deny it?

CAVARADOSSI
I deny it and I love you!
(tries to kiss her)

TOSCA
(with gentle reproach)
Oh! Before the Madonna,
No, my dear Mario,
Let me first pray to her and offert flowers.
(She slowly approaches the Madonna, artfully arranges the flowers she has brought around her, kneels and prays with great devotion, crossing herself; then she rises and goes toward Cavaradossi, who has meanwhile started to resume his work.)
Now listen - tonight I am singing,
But the programme will be short. You wait for me
At the stage entrance, 
and we shall go to your villa, all alone.

CAVARADOSSI
(his thoughts still elsewhere)
Tonight!

TOSCA
It's a full moon
and the nightly release of floral scents
inebriates the heart! - Aren't you happy?
(sits on the steps near Cavaradossi)

CAVARADOSSI
(still somewhat distracted and thoughtful)
Very!

TOSCA
(struck by his tone)
Say it again!

CAVARADOSSI
Very!

TOSCA
(irritated)
You say it poorly:
Do you not long for our little house
That, hidden in the grove, awaits us?
The nest sacred to us, unknown to the world,
Full of love and mystery?
By your side, listening 
to the voices of the night
rising along the silent 
starry shadows!...
From the woods and from the hedges,
from the dry grasses, from the depths
of shattered graves
scented with thyme,
by night whispers come forth
of tiny cupids 
and perfidious counsels
that soften the hearts.
Bloom, oh vast fields, palpitate
oh sea winds, in the moon's pale glimmer,
rain pleasures, oh starry vaults!
Mad love burns for Tosca!
(resting her head on Cavaradossi's shoulder)

CAVARADOSSI
(defeated, but vigilant)
You bind me in your snares,
My siren, my siren, I will come!
(looks towards the side from where Angelotti went out)
Now leave me to my work

TOSCA
(surprised)
You dismiss me?

CAVARADOSSI
My work is pressing, you know it!

TOSCA
(irritated, getting up)
I leave! I leave!
(she moves a little away from Cavaradossi, then turns to look at him, sees the painting, and, in a state of great agitation, returns to Cavaradossi)
And who is that 
blond woman over there?

CAVARADOSSI
(calm)
Mary Magdalene. 
Do you like her?

TOSCA
She is too beautiful!

CAVARADOSSI
(laughing and bowing)
Precious praise!

TOSCA
(suspicious)
You laugh?
Those sky-blue eyes, I've seen them before...

CAVARADOSSI
(unconcerned)
There are so many in the world!

TOSCA
(trying to remember)
Wait... wait...
(steps onto the platform triumphantly)
It's Attavanti!

CAVARADOSSI
(laughing)
Brava!...

TOSCA
(overcome by jealousy)
Do you see her? Does she love you?
(crying)
Do you love her?..

CAVARADOSSI
(try to calm her down)
It was pure chance...

TOSCA
(not listening, with jealous anger)
Those steps and those whispers...
Ah! She was here just now!

CAVARADOSSI
Come here!

TOSCA
The shameless flirt! 
(threatening)
Give her to me!

CAVARADOSSI
(serious)
I saw her yesterday, but it was pure chance...
She came here to pray...
And I, unnoticed, painted her picture.

TOSCA
Swear!

CAVARADOSSI
(serious)
I swear!

TOSCA
(her eyes still on the painting)
How intently she stares at me!

CAVARADOSSI
(gently urges her to step down the flight of stairs. She descends backwards, keeping her hands raised holding Cavaradossi’s. As she descends, Tosca keeps her face turned toward the painting, while Mario has his back to it.)
Come away!

TOSCA
She taunts me, laughing.
(they descended)

CAVARADOSSI
Foolishness!
(holding her close and gazing at her)

TOSCA
(with a gentle reproach)
Ah, those eyes...

CAVARADOSSI
What eyes in the world 
can be a match
for your dark burning eyes?
It is in them that my whole being fastens,
Eyes soft with love and fierce with rage!
What other eyes in the world can match
your dark eyes!

TOSCA
(won over, resting her head on his shoulder)
Oh, how well you know the art
Of making yourself loved!
(mischievously)
But... give her dark eyes!...

CAVARADOSSI
(tenderly)
My jealous Tosca!

TOSCA
Yes, I feel it... I torment you
Unceasingly.

CAVARADOSSI
My jealous Tosca!

TOSCA
I am certain of your forgiveness
if you see my sorrow!

CAVARADOSSI
My worshipped Tosca,
All things in you delight me;
Your audacious wrath
And your pangs of love!

TOSCA
Say them again
Those comforting words...
Say them again!

CAVARADOSSI
My life, my restless lover.
I shall always say, "I love you, Floria!"
Ah! Temper your soul,
I shall always say "I love you!"

TOSCA
(softening, fearing she is overcome)
God! What a sin!
You have undone my hair!

CAVARADOSSI
Now go, leave me!

TOSCA
You stay at your work 
until this evening. And promise: 
be it chance or fortune,
blond or dark locks,
no woman will come praying!

CAVARADOSSI
I swear it, beloved... Go!

TOSCA
How you hurry me along!

CAVARADOSSI
(with a gentle reproach, seeing jealousy resurface)
Again?

TOSCA
(falling into his arms, presenting her cheek)
No, forgive me!...

CAVARADOSSI
(jokingly)
Before the Madonna?

TOSCA
(gesturing towards the Madonna)
She is so good!
(They kiss. As she starts to leave, glancing back at the painting, she says to him mischievously:)
But give her dark eyes!
(She flees quickly; Cavaradossi remains, moved and pensive.)

SIXTH SCENE

(As soon as Tosca has left, Cavaradossi listens to her receding footsteps; then, cautiously, he opens the door a crack and looks out. Seeing that all is quiet, he runs to the chapel. Angelotti appears just behind the gate.)

CAVARADOSSI
(opening the gate for Angelotti, who has of course heard the foregoing dialogue)
She is good, my Tosca, but, faithful
She hides nothing to the confessor, so I kept quiet.
It is wiser so.

ANGELOTTI
Are we alone?

CAVARADOSSI
Yes, What is your plan?...

ANGELOTTI
As things stand now, either to flee the State
Or stay in hiding in Rome... My sister...

CAVARADOSSI
Attavanti?

ANGELOTTI
Yes... She hid some women's 
clothes under the altar there...
A dress, a veil, a fan...
(looks around with fear)
As soon as it gets dark
I'll put these garments on...

CAVARADOSSI
Now I understand!
That circumspect behaviour
And that fervent prayer
In so young and beautitul a woman
Had made me suspect
Some secret love!
Now I understand!
It was the love of a sister!

ANGELOTTI
She has dared all
To save me from the wicked Scarpia!

CAVARADOSSI
Scarpia?! That bigoted [[satyr|Denotes rough and uncouth manners as well as morbid lust]] who hones
Libertine [[frenzy|In particular related to lust]]
with practices of devotion
and for his wanton appetites
(with growing force)
employs the confessor and the executioner!
May it cost my life, I will save you!
But to hesitate until night is unsafe...

ANGELOTTI
I fear the sunlight!...

CAVARADOSSI
(pointing)
The Chapel gives 
On a vegetable garden, beyond that is a canefield
That winds along through meadows to my villa.

ANGELOTTI
I know [[it|The villa.]]...

CAVARADOSSI
Here is the key... Before evening
I shall join you, - Take with you
The woman's garments.

ANGELOTTI
(bundling together the clothes from under the altar)
Should I put them on?

CAVARADOSSI
You need not now, the path's deserted...

ANGELOTTI
(about to go)
Good-bye!

CAVARADOSSI
(running towards Angelotti)
If there's sign of danger, run
To the garden well. There's water at the bottom,
But half-way down, a little passage
Leads to a dark room, 
Safe and impenetrable refuge!

(The report of a cannon. The two men look at each other in alarm.)

ANGELOTTI
The cannon of the castle!...

CAVARADOSSI
Your escape was discovered! 
Now Scarpia unleashes his cops!

ANGELOTTI
Good-bye!

CAVARADOSSI
(with sudden resolve)
I will come with you! We must be on guard!

ANGELOTTI
I hear someone!

CAVARADOSSI
(with enthusiasm)
If they assault us, to battle!

(They leave quickly by the Chapel.)

SEVENTH SCENE

SACRISTAN
(enters running, frenzied, shouting:)
Immense joy, Excellency!...
(looks towards the scaffold, and is surprised that once again the painter is not there)
He's gone! That pains me!...
He who aggrieves a misbeliever
Earns an indulgence!
(Priests, pupils and singers of the Chapel. All enter tumultuously from every direction)
The whole choir is here!
Hurry!...
(other pupils arrive tardily, and eventually all group  together.)

STUDENTS
(in great confusion)
Where?

SACRISTAN
(pushing some of the priests along)
In the sacristy...

SOME PUPILS
But what happened?

SACRISTAN
You don't know?
(panting)
Bonaparte... the villain...
Bonaparte...

OTHERS
(they approach the sacristan and crowd around him, while others rush over and join the first group)
Well? What happened?

SACRISTAN
He was plucked, quartered,
And thrown to [[Beelzebub|The devil]]!

STUDENTS, SINGERS
Who says so? 
It’s a dream! 
It’s nonsense!

SACRISTAN
It is truthful word;
The news just came!

CHORUS
Let's celebrate the victory!

SACRISTAN
And tonight
A mighty torch procession,
A gala evening at Farnese Palace,
And a specially composed 
new cantata
With Floria Tosca!...
And in the churches,
Hymns to the Lord!
Now go and get dressed,
And no more shouting!
Go... go... to the Sacristy!

CHORUS
(laughing and shouting joyfully, paying no heed to the Sacristan, who is vainly shoving them toward the sacristy)
Double pay... Te Deum... Gloria!
Long live the King!... Let's celebrate the victory!

EIGHTH SCENE
(Scarpia - Sacristan - Choristers - Choirboys, etc. - Spoletta - Police agents. Their shouting and laughter are at their peak when an ironic voice abruptly cuts short the vulgar clamor of singing and laughter. It is Scarpia; behind him are Spoletta and several police agents.)

SCARPIA
(with great authority)
Such a racket in church! A fine show of respect!

SACRISTAN
(stammering with fear)
Excellency! The great rejoicing...

SCARPIA
Prepare for the Te Deum.
(all depart crest-fallen; even the Sacristan tries to slip away, but Scarpia brusquely holds him)
You stay here!

SACRISTAN
(cowering)
I shan't move!

SCARPIA
(to Spoletta)
And you, search every corner,
Gather every clue.

SPOLETTA
Very well.
(signals two cops to follow him)

SCARPIA
(to other cops following him)
Keep watch at the doors,
Without arousing suspicion!
(to Sacristan)
Now, as for you! Weigh 
Your answers well. A prisoner of the State
Has just escaped from Castel Sant'Angelo...
(forceful)
He took refuge here...

SACRISTAN
Heaven help us!

SCARPIA
He may still be here. 
Where is the Chapel of the Attavanti?

SACRISTAN
That is it there,
(goes to the gate and finds it half-open)
It's open! Archangels!
And there's another key!

SCARPIA
A good clue... Let's go in.
(They enter the chapel, then return: Scarpia, highly annoyed, holds a closed fan in his hands, nervously waving it back and forth.)
It was a bad mistake
To fire that cannon! The scoundrel
has taken flight, but left behind precious...
booty... a fan.
(waving it in the air)
Who was the accomplice who set up this misdeed?
(remains deep in thought, then looks closely at the fan; suddenly, he spots a coat of arms and exclaims excitedly:)
The Marchesa Attavanti! 
Her crest...
(looks around scrutinizing every corner of the church: his gaze rests on the scaffold, the painter's tools, the painting... and he recognises the familiar features of the Attavanti in the face of the saint)
Her portrait!
(to the Sacristan)
Who painted that picture?

SACRISTAN
(ever more fearful)
Cavalier 
Cavaradossi...

SCARPIA
Him!
(one of the policemen who followed Scarpia, returns from the Chapel carrying the basket that Cavaradossi gave to Angelotti)

SACRISTAN
(looking at it)
Heavens! The basket!

SCARPIA
(pursuing his own thoughts)
Him! Tosca's lover! 
A suspicious man!
A Voltairian!

SACRISTAN
(having examined the basket, exclaims in great surprise:)
Empty?... Empty!...

SCARPIA
What did you say?
(on seeing policeman with the basket)
What happened?...

SACRISTAN
(taking the basket)
They found this basket
In the Chapel.

SCARPIA
Have you recognise this?

SACRISTAN
Yes, indeed!
(hesitant and fearful)
It's the painter's basket... but... even so...

SCARPIA
Spit out what you know!

SACRISTAN
(increasingly frightened and almost in tears, he shows him the empty basket)
I left it for him filled
With delicious food...
The painter's meal!...

SCARPIA
(attentive, seeking to discover more)
He must have eaten!

SACRISTAN
In the chapel?
(gesturing "no" with his hand)
He had no key,
Nor did he want to eat... He told me so himself.
So I put the basket... 
safely to one side.
(Shows where he put the basket, and leaves it there.)
[[Libera me domine|Set me free, Lord]]!

(pause)

SCARPIA
(to himself)
It's all clear now
The Sacristan's food
Was Angelotti's booty!
(sees Tosca, who enters in haste)
Tosca? She must not see me.
(As soon as he sees Tosca enter, he skillfully hides behind the column where the holy water stoup stands, imperiously signaling the Sacristan to stay put; the latter, trembling and flustered, makes his way toward the painter's scaffolding.)
To drive a jealous man to desperation,
[[Iago|From Shakespeare's Othello.]] had a handkerchief... and I, a fan!...

NINETH SCENE
(Tosca - Scarpia - Sagrestano)

TOSCA
(goes straight to the platform, but not finding Cavaradossi there, and still in a state of great agitation, she goes to look for him in the main nave of the church.)
Mario?! Mario?!

SACRISTAN
(at the foot of the scaffold, approaching Tosca)
The painter Cavaradossi? 
Who knows where he is?
He vanished, scuttled away
By his own witchcraft.
(he slips away)

TOSCA
Deceived? No!... No!...
He cannot betray me!
(almost crying)

SCARPIA
(turns the corner and appears before Tosca, who is startled by his sudden arrival. He dips his fingers into the stoup and offers her the holy water; outside, church bells are ringing.)
Kind Tosca, my hand
Awaits yours, your little hand,
Not for gallantry,
But to offer you Holy Water.

TOSCA
(touches Scarpias fingers and crosses herself)
Thank you, Sir!

SCARPIA
Yours is a noble
example.
Filled with holy zeal, you draw
From Heaven the mastery of art
That revives faith.

TOSCA
(distraught and preoccupied)
You are too kind...
(some commoners begin to enter the church and make their way toward the back)

SCARPIA
Pious women are so rare...
You step on the stage...
(with intent)
Yet you come to church to pray...

TOSCA
(surprised)
What do you mean?...

SCARPIA
And do not act like certain brazen women
who share the face and ways
(pointing to the portrait)
of Magdalene...
(with marked emphasis)
and carry on love affairs!

TOSCA
(snaps alert)
What? Love affairs? Your proof!

SCARPIA
(showing her the fan)
Is this a painter's tool?

TOSCA
(grabbing it)
A fan! Where was it?

(some peasants enter)

SCARPIA
There on the scaffold. Clearly
Somebody surprised the lovers,
And she lost her feathers in her flight!

TOSCA
(studying the fan)
The crown! The crest! It’s Attavanti's!
Oh foreboding suspicion!...

SCARPIA
(to himself)
I've hit the mark!

TOSCA
(forgetting both the place and Scarpia, tries to hold back her tears)
And I came to him all sorrowful
to tell him: in vain tonight
[[the sky darkens|Referring to her poem above, in which she suggested they would spend the night together at the villa under the light of the full moon]]...
Lovestruck Tosca
is a [[prisoner... of royal festivities|She has to sing for the celebrations, as mentioned previously]].

(a group of shepherds and peasant women enters)

SCARPIA
(to himself)
The poison has already gnawed at her!
(mellifluously, to Tosca)
What bothers you,
sweet lady?...
A rebel 
tear falls
along the pretty
cheeks and wets them;
Sweet lady,
what troubles you?

TOSCA
It is nothing!

(various noblemen accompany some ladies)

SCARPIA
(with marked intent)
I would give my life
To wipe away those tears.

TOSCA
(unheeding)
Here I pine away, while 
In another's arms she mocks my longing!

SCARPIA
(to himself)
The poison is biting!

(some townspeople enter, one by one)

TOSCA
(with great bitterness)
Where are they? If only
I could catch them, the traitors!
(growing increasingly furious)
Oh, what a suspicion!
That villa is a haven
for duplicitous loves!
(with immense anguish)
Traitor!
Oh, my beautiful [[nest|Resumes the aviary metaphor, see "civetta" (little owl) with reference to Ms. Attavanti and the lost "piume" (feathers) of the fleeing Angelotti earlier.]], defiled with filth!
(with sudden resolve)
I shall swoop down upon them unawares!
(turning threateningly toward the portrait)
You shall not have him tonight. I swear it!

SCARPIA
(with a scandalized air and tone of rebuke)
In church!

TOSCA
God will pardon me... He sees me weeping!
(weeps bitterly; Scarpia supports her as he leads her to the exit, feigning reassurance. As soon as Tosca has left, the church gradually fills with people. The crowd gathers at the back, awaiting the Cardinal; some are kneeling in prayer.)

SCARPIA
(after escorting Tosca, he returns to the column and makes a signal; Spoletta immediately appears)
Three cops... a carriage... 
Quick!... follow her
Wherever she goes!... Unseen!... Take care of it!

SPOLETTA
Very well! The meeting place?

SCARPIA
Palazzo Farnese!
(Spoletta quickly sets off with three cops, wearing a sardonic smile)
Go, Tosca! Scarpia digs a [[nest|See the reference to her nest ("nido") Tosca made above]] within your heart!
It is Scarpia who looses in flight
The falcon of your jealousy.
What promise lies in your ready suspicion!

(The procession accompanying the Cardinal to the high altar emerges: Swiss Guards clear a path through the crowd, which forms two lines. Scarpia bows and prays as the Cardinal passes. The Cardinal blesses the crowd, who bow reverently.)

CHORUS
Adjutorum nostrum in nomine Domini
Qui fecit coelum et terram
Sit nomen Domini benedictum
[[Et hoc nunc et usque in saeculum.|Our help is in the name of the Lord, Who made heaven and earth. Blessed be the name of the Lord, Both now and forevermore.]]

SCARPIA
(fiercely)
A twofold aim
guides my will; nor is the rebel's head
the most precious prize. Ah, to see the flame
of those triumphant eyes
(with erotic passion)
grow languid with the throes of love,
within my arms...
(fiercely)
The one to the gallows,
the other in my arms...
(He stands motionless, staring into the void. The entire crowd is turned toward the high altar; some are kneeling.)

CHORUS
Te Deum laudamus,
[[Te Deum confitemur|You God, we worship, You God, we acknowledge]]!

SCARPIA
(as though awakening him from a dream)
[[Tosca, you make me forget God|Climax of the aria]]!
(he kneels and prays devoutly)

CHORUS, CHORUS
Te aeternum
[[Patrem omnis terra veneratur|You, eternal father, the whole earth venerates]]!

ACT TWO

(Scarpia's room on the upper floor of the Palazzo Farnese. A table is set for a meal. A large window overlooks the palace courtyard. It is night.)

SCENE ONE

SCARPIA
(He is seated at the table, dining. He pauses occasionally to reflect. He glances at the clock; he is restless and pensive.)
Tosca is a good falcon!...
Surely by this time
My hounds are sinking their fangs into the two prey!
And tomorrow's dawn will see
on the scaffold
Angelotti and the fine Mario hanging 
from a noose.
(rings a bell. Enter Sciarrone)
Is Tosca in the palace?...

SCIARRONE
A chamberlain has just gone
To look for her...

SCARPIA
(points towards the window)
Open the window. It is late.
(from the floor below — where the Queen of Naples, Maria Carolina, is giving a grand ball in honor of Melas — the sound of an orchestra can be heard)
The performance is still missing its Diva,
And they strum [gavottes|A type dance music].
(to Sciarrone)
You shall wait for Tosca at the entrance:
Tell her I shall expect her
After the performance...
(Sciarrone begins to leave)
Better yet...
(rises and goes to write a note hurriedly)
Give her this note.
(exit Sciarrone. Scarpia returns to the table and, pouring himself a drink, says:)
She will come... for love of her Mario!
And for love of Mario... she will yield
To my pleasure. Such is the profound misery
Of profound loves... Violent conquest
Has stronger relish 
than mellifluous surrender.
I take no delight in sighs 
and [[milky lunar dawns|Incidentally evokes Tosca's poem to Mario in act one, which Scarpia did not hear]]. I know not how to draw
Harmony from guitars, or horoscopes from flowers, 
(indignantly)
nor am I apt at giving [[languid glances|Literally "making fish eyes"]],
Or cooing like a dove. 
(stands, but does not move far from the table)
I crave. I pursue 
what I crave, sate myself and cast it away...
Then seek new bait. God made diverse beauties
As he made diverse wines... and of these
divine works I mean to taste my full.
(drinks)

SCIARRONE
(enters)
Spoletta's here.

SCARPIA
(extremely excited, shouting)
Show him in. In good time, too.

(Sciarrone goes out to call Spoletta, who he accompanies into the room, then remaining near the door at the back.)

SECOND SCENE

SCARPIA
(He sits down and, fully occupied with his supper, questions Spoletta without looking at him.)
Oh kind man, how was the hunt?...

SPOLETTA
(moving forward a little and fearfully)
Saint Ignatius help me!
We kept on the lady's trail.
Followed her to a solitary villa
lost in the woods...
She entered it. And soon after came out alone.
Then I swiftly climb 
the garden wall with my dogs and
Burst into the house.

SCARPIA
Well done, Spoletta!

SPOLETTA
(hesitant)
I sniff!... I scratch!... I rummage!...

SCARPIA
(sensing Spolettas hesitation, rises scowling, pale with anger, with furrowed brows)
Ah! Angelotti?...

SPOLETTA
Nowhere to be found

SCARPIA
(in a rage)
Ah dog! Traitor!
Face of a snake,
(shouting)
To the gallows!

SPOLETTA
(trembling, trying to avert Scarpia's wrath)
Jesus!
(timidly)
The painter was there...

SCARPIA
(interrupting)
Cavaradossi?

SPOLETTA
(nods and quickly adds:)
He knows where the other is hiding...
His every gesture,
his every word betrayed
such scornful irony,
that I took him under arrest!

SCARPIA
(with a sigh of satisfaction)
Thank goodness!

SPOLETTA
(waving towards the antechamber)
He is there.

(Scarpia paces up and down, pondering; he stops abruptly; he hears, through the open window, the choral cantata being sung in the Queen's apartment.)

TOSCA and CHORUS
The song of humanity rises and ascends,
traversing spaces, traversing heavens,
through unknown [[empyrean suns|In Aristotelian cosmology, the highest sphere of the universe]],
foretold by the gospels,
to arrive to you, oh king of kings,
may this song fly to you.
To you may this hymn fly,
supreme God of victory.
God, who existed before the ages
among the songs of the angels
may this hymn of glory
now fly to you!
The song of humanity rises and ascends,
traversing spaces, traversing heavens,
to arrive to you, oh king of kings.

SCARPIA
So Tossca came back - she is down there below him...
(he has an idea and quickly tells Spoletta:)
Bring in the Cavalier.
(Exit Spoletta. To Sciarrone:)
Get me
Roberti and the Judge.

(Exit Sciarrone. Scarpia sits down again.)

THIRD SCENE
(Spoletta and four policemen bring in Mario Cavaradossi. Then enter Roberti, the executor of justice, the Judge with a scribe, and Sciarrone.)

CAVARADOSSI
(disdainful, advancing impetuously)
Such violence!...

SCARPIA
(with studied courtesy)
Cavalier, please be seated...

CAVARADOSSI
I want to know...

SCARPIA
(indicating a chair at the other side of the table)
Be seated...

CAVARADOSSI
(declining)
I’ll stand.

SCARPIA
So be it!
(looks straight at Cavaradossi, before asking him)
Are you aware that a prisoner...

(Tosca's voice is heard in the cantata)

CAVARADOSSI
(moved)
Her voice!...

SCARPIA
(who had paused on hearing Tosca's voice, resumes)
...Are you aware that a prisoner
Fled today from Castel Sant'Angelo?

CAVARADOSSI
I am not aware.

SCARPIA
Yet it is reported
That you sheltered him in Sant'Andrea,
provided him
with food and clothing...

CAVARADOSSI
(unflinching)
Lies!

SCARPIA
(keeping calm)
...And took him
To a suburban property of yours...

CAVARADOSSI
I deny. What evidence?

SCARPIA
(melliflously)
A faithful servant...

CAVARADOSSI
Get to the facts! Who is my accuser? 
(ironically)
In vain your henchmen ransacked my villa.

SCARPIA
Proof that he is well hidden.

CAVARADOSSI
Suspicions of a spy!

SPOLETTA
(offended, intervenes)
He laughed at our search...

CAVARADOSSI
And still I laugh!

SCARPIA
(harshly, rising)
This is a place for tears!
(threatening)
Beware!
(very nervously)
Enough now! Answer me!
(irritated and disturbed by the voices from the cantata, he goes to close the window; then, imperiously, he turns to Cavaradossi:)
Where is Angelotti?

CAVARADOSSI
I don't know.

SCARPIA
You deny gaving him food?

CAVARADOSSI
I deny it.

SCARPIA
And clothes?

CAVARADOSSI
I deny it.

SCARPIA
And refuge in your villa?
And that he is hidden there?

CAVARADOSSI
(vehemently)
I deny it! I deny it!

SCARPIA
(almost paternally, calming down again)
Come, Cavalier, reflect:
This stubborness of yours is not prudent.
Great anguish a prompt confession
will avert! I advise you, tell:
Where, then, is Angelotti?

CAVARADOSSI
I do not know.

SCARPIA
Again,
For the last time: where is he?

CAVARADOSSI
I do not know.

SPOLETTA
(to himself)
Oh, fine stretches of rope!

FOURTH SCENE

(Enter Tosca breathless)

SCARPIA
(to himself)
Here she is!

TOSCA
(sees Cavaradossi, and runs to embrace him)
Mario?! You, here?

CAVARADOSSI
(in a low voice)
Of what you saw there, say nothing, else you kill me!

(Tosca indicates she understands)

SCARPIA
(solemnly)
Mario Cavaradossi,
The judge awaits your testimony.
(to Roberti)
First, the usual formalities... Then... as I shall order...

(signals to Sciarrone to open the door leading to the torture chamber. The Judge enters, followed by the others, leaving Tosca and Scarpia behind. Spoletta withdraws to the door at the far end of the room.)

SCIARRONE
(closes the door. Tosca shows great surprise; Scarpia, with studied courtesy, reassures her)

SCARPIA
(gallantly)
And now between us, like good friends.
Out with that look of dismay...
(beckons Tosca to be seated)

TOSCA
(with studied calm)
I feel no dismay...

SCARPIA
What about the fan?
(passes behind the sofa where Tosca is sitting and leans upon it. He still adopts a gallant air.)

TOSCA
(with feigned indifference)
It was foolish jealousy...

SCARPIA
So, Ms. Attavanti was not at the villa?

TOSCA
No: he was alone.

SCARPIA
Alone? 
(inquiring with malice)
Are you quite sure of it?

TOSCA
Nothing evades the jealous. Alone! Alone!
(with petulant persistence)

SCARPIA
(takes a chair, brings it in front of Tosca, sits on it and looks at her fixedly)
Really?

TOSCA
(annoyed)
Yes, alone!

SCARPIA
You protest too much! 
Perhaps you fear you may betray yourself.
(turning towards the doorway of the torture chamber and calling out)
Sciarrone, what says the Cavalier?

SCIARRONE
(appearing in the doorway)
He denies.

SCARPIA
(raising his voice, towards the open door)
Keep pressing him!

(Sciarrone re-enters the torture chamber, closing the door)

TOSCA
(laughing)
Oh, it's useless!

SCARPIA
(serious, pacing back and forth)
We shall see, Madam.

TOSCA
(slowly, with an ironic smile)
So, to please you, one ought to 
lie?

SCARPIA
No, but the truth might spare him
a truly painful hour...

TOSCA
(surprised)
A painful hour? What do you mean?
What is happening in that room?

SCARPIA
It is by force that the law
is carried out.

TOSCA
Oh, God!... What's happening?!

SCARPIA
(with an expression of ferocity and with increasing force)
Bound hand and foot,
Your lover has a hooked ring 
Around his temples,
From which blood spurts at every denial
With no mercy!

TOSCA
(jumps to her feet)
It's not true! It's not true!
A demon's sneer...
(listens with great anxiety, hands nervously clutching the back of the sofa)

CAVARADOSSI'S VOICE
Alas!
(a prolonged groan)

TOSCA
A groan? Mercy! Mercy!

SCARPIA
It is up to you to save him.

TOSCA
So be it... But stop it!

SCARPIA
(goes over to the door)
Sciarrone,
Release!

SCIARRONE
(appears in the doorway)
Everything?

SCARPIA
Everything.
(Sciarrone returns to the torture chamber, shutting the door)
And now the truth...

TOSCA
Let me see him!

SCARPIA
No!

TOSCA
(managing to get near the door)
Mario!

CAVARADOSSI’S VOICE
(painfully)
Tosca!

TOSCA
Are they hurting you
still?

CAVARADOSSI’S VOICE
No - Courage! Be silent - I scorn the pain!

SCARPIA
(approaching Tosca)
Come on, Tosca, speak!

TOSCA
(strengthened by Mario’s words)
I know nothing!

SCARPIA
Wasn't that enough for you?
Roberti, start again...
(begins to move towards thee door)

TOSCA
(throwing herself in front of the door, to keep him from giving the order)
No! Stop!

SCARPIA
Will you speak?

TOSCA
No... monster!
You torment him... you're killing him!

SCARPIA
It's your silence
That torments him much more.

TOSCA
You laugh...
At this ghastly torment?

SCARPIA
(with enthusiasm)
Tosca on stage
Was never more tragic!

TOSCA
(horrified, she moves away from Scarpia, who, seized by a sudden fit of savagery, turns to Spoletta)

SCARPIA
(shouting)
Open the door so she
May hear his groans!

(Spoletta opens the door and stands stiffly on the threshold.)

CAVARADOSSI’S VOICE
I defy you!

SCARPIA
(shouting at Roberti)
Harder! Harder!

CAVARADOSSI’S VOICE
I defy you!

SCARPIA
(to Tosca)
Speak now...

TOSCA
What can I say?

SCARPIA
Come on!

TOSCA
Ah! I know nothing!
(despairing)
Should I lie?

SCARPIA
(insisting)
Tell me, where is Angelotti? Speak!
Come now  — where is he hiding?

TOSCA
No! — Ah! I can bear no more! — What horror!
End the torment! The suffering is too great!

CAVARADOSSI’S VOICE
Ah!

TOSCA
(She turns once more imploringly to Scarpia, who signals Spoletta to let Tosca approach; she goes to the open doorway and, horrified by the sight of the ghastly scene, turns to Cavaradossi in utter anguish:)
Mario, will you allow
that I speak?

CAVARADOSSI’S VOICE
(broken)
No, no.

TOSCA
(pleading)
Listen, I can bear no more...

CAVARADOSSI’S VOICE
Fool, what do you know? What can you say?

SCARPIA
(furious at Cavaradossi’s words and fearing that they might further encourage Tosca to remain silent, he shouts terrifyingly at Spoletta:)
Shut him up!

(Spoletta goes into the torture chamber, returning after a moment. Tosca overcome with emotion, has fallen prostrate on the sofa. Sobbing she appeals to Scarpia. He stands silent and impassive. Spoletta, meanwhile, mumbles a prayer under his breath:)

TOSCA
What have I done to you in my life?
It is I you torture so!...
You torture my soul...
(bursts into sobs, whispering)
Yes, my soul you are torturing!

SPOLETTA
(muttering in a posture of prayer)
Judex ergo, cum sedebit,
Quidquid latet apparebit,
[[Nil inultum remanebit|From  Dies Irae: When the Judge is seated, Whatever is hidden shall be revealed, Nothing shall remain unavenged]].

(Taking advantage of Tosca’s prostration, Scarpia moves toward the torture chamber and signals for the torment to resume—a horrible scream is heard—Tosca springs to her feet and immediately, in a stifled voice, says rapidly to Scarpia:)

TOSCA
In the well... in the garden...

SCARPIA
Angelotti is there?...

TOSCA
(muffled)
Yes.

SCARPIA
(loudly, towards the torture chamber)
Enough, Roberti!

SCIARRONE
(re-opening the door)
He fainted!

TOSCA
(to Scarpia)
Murderer!
I want to see him.

SCARPIA
Bring him here...

(Sciarrone re-enters, and immediately the unconscious Cavaradossi appears, carried by the guards who lay him on the couch. Tosca rushes to him, but the horror of seeing her lover bloodied is so overwhelming that, in dismay, she covers her face to avoid looking at him — then, ashamed of this weakness, she kneels beside him, kissing him and weeping. Sciarrone, the Judge, Roberti, and the Scribe exit at the back, while, at a signal from Scarpia, Spoletta and the guards halt.)

CAVARADOSSI
(as he comes to)
Floria!

TOSCA
(covering him with kisses)
Beloved...

CAVARADOSSI
It is you?

TOSCA
(warmly)
How you have suffered,
My soul!... But the just
God will punish him!

CAVARADOSSI
Tosca, Did you speak?

TOSCA
No, my love...

CAVARADOSSI
Really?

SCARPIA
(loudly to Spoletta)
In the well
of the garden. Go, Spoletta!

(Spoletta exits; Cavaradossi, having overheard, rises threateningly toward Tosca; then his strength fails him and he sinks onto the sofa, exclaiming reproachfully and bitterly to Tosca:)

CAVARADOSSI
You have betrayed me!

TOSCA
(beseeching)
Mario!

CAVARADOSSI
(pushing away Tosca, who is clinging tightly to him)
Damn you!

SCIARRONE
(suddenly bursting in, breathless)
Excellency! What news!...

SCARPIA
(taken aback)
What does that pitiful look mean?

SCIARRONE
News of defeat...

SCARPIA
What defeat? How? Where? 

SCIARRONE
At Marengo...

SCARPIA
(impatiently, shouting)
Blockhead!

SCIARRONE
Bonaparte is victor!

SCARPIA
Melas...

SCIARRONE
No! Melas is fleeing!...

(Cavaradossi, having listened to Sciarrone’s words with mounting anxiety, finds the strength in his own fervor to rise threateningly before Scarpia.)

CAVARADOSSI
Victory! Victory!
[[The avenging dawn rises|The register is lifted along with Cavaradossi's spirit]]
To make the wicked tremble!
Liberty rises, down fall tyrannies!
In the suffering of my martyrdom
You will see me rejoice...
Your heart trembles, o Scarpia, butcher!

(Tosca, clinging desperately to Cavaradossi, attempts, with broken words, to silence him.)

TOSCA
Mario, be quiet, have pity on me!

SCARPIA
(stares cynically at Cavaradossi)
Boast, shout! - Hasten to 
reveal to me the dregs 
of your guilty soul!
Go! - Moribund,
The noose awaits you!
(and irritated by Cavaradossi's words, he shouts to the guards:)
Take him away from me!
(Sciarrone and the guards seize Cavaradossi and drag him toward the door — Tosca, with a supreme effort, tries to cling to Cavaradossi, but in vain: she is brutally thrust aside.)

TOSCA
Mario... with you...
(The guards lead Cavaradossi away; Sciarrone follows them. Tosca rushes to follow Cavaradossi, but Scarpia positions himself in front of the door and closes it, pushing Tosca back.)

SCARPIA
Not you!

FITH SCENE

TOSCA
(like a whimper)
Save him!

SCARPIA
Me?... You rather!
(approaches the table, sees his half-finished dinner, and returns, calm and smiling)
My poor supper was interrupted.
(sees Tosca, dejected and motionless, still at the door)
So dejected?... Come now, my fair lady,
Sit down here. Shall we try to find
Together a way to save him?
(Tosca rouses herself and looks at him; Scarpia continues to smile and sits down, simultaneously motioning for Tosca to sit.)
Well then... sit down... and let's chat...
(polishes a glass with a napkin, then holds it up to the light of the candelabra)
And first, 
a drink. It's wine from Spain.
(fills the glass and offers it to Tosca)
A drink 
(kindly)
to hearten you.

TOSCA
(She sits facing Scarpia, gazing fixedly at him. Resting her elbows on the table, she supports her face with her hands and, in a tone of the deepest scorn, asks Scarpia:)
How much?

SCARPIA
(imperturbable, as he pours himself a drink)
How much?

TOSCA
Your price!...

SCARPIA
(laughs)
Indeed — they call me venal, but to a beautiful woman
(insinuatingly and pointedly)
I do not sell myself for mere currency.
If I must betray
my sworn faith... I want different goods.
I have waited for this hour!
Already in the past I burned
With passion for the Diva!
But just a moment ago I beheld you
as I had never seen you before!
(extremely excited, stands up)
Those tears of yours were lava
To my senses and that look
That shot darts of hatred against me, 
Inflamed my desires!...
Agile as a leopard
You grabbed your lover;
Ah! In that instant
I vowed you would be mine!...
Mine!
(approaches, reaching out his arms toward Tosca; she, having listened motionless and petrified to Scarpia’s lustful words, suddenly springs up and takes refuge behind the sofa.)

TOSCA
Ah!

SCARPIA
(quasi inseguendola)
Yes, I  will have you!...

TOSCA
(horrified, runs to the window)
I would sooner throw myself down!

SCARPIA
(coldly)
I hold your Mario
as a pledge!...

TOSCA
Ah! Wretch...
the horrible bargain!
(the idea of going to the Queen flashes through her mind, and she runs toward the door)

SCARPIA
(guessing his thoughts, steps aside)
I will do you no violence. You are free.
You may go.
(Tosca, with a cry of joy, moves to leave; Scarpia holds her back with a gesture and an ironic laugh)
But it is a vain hope... the Queen
would grant a pardon only to a corpse!
(Tosca recoils in terror and, staring at Scarpia, sinks onto the sofa; then she tears her gaze away from him with a look of utter disgust and hatred)
How you hate me!
(with conviction and self-satisfaction)

TOSCA
(with utmost hatred and contempt)
Ah! God!...

SCARPIA
(approaching)
This is how I want you!

TOSCA
(exasperated)
Don't touch me, demon! 
I hate you, hate you, you base, foul wretch!
(flees from Scarpia in horror)

SCARPIA
What does it matter?!
(moving even closer to her)
Spasms of wrath... spasms of passion!

TOSCA
Wretch!

SCARPIA
(trying to seize her)
Mine!

TOSCA
(retreats behind the table)
Wretch!

SCARPIA
(pursuing her)
Mine!

TOSCA
Help! Help!
(A distant roll of drums draws slowly near, then fades again into the distance.)

SCARPIA
(halting)
Do you hear?
It is the drum. It begins. It leads the way
For the last march of the condemned. Time passes!
(Tosca listens in terrible dread, and then comes back from the window to lean exhausted on the sofa.)
Do you know... what dreadful work is being carried out
Down there? 
There... They are raising the gallows!
(Tosca makes a gesture of despair and terror)
By your wish,
Your Mario has but one hour left to live.
(coldly leans against the corner of the table, continuing to watch Tosca. Tosca, overcome with grief, sinks onto the sofa. Scarpia coldly goes to lean against the corner of the table, pours himself some coffee, and sips it while continuing to watch Tosca.)

TOSCA
(in deepest grief)
[[I lived for art, I lived for love|Also "I lived on art, I lived on love", meaning "my life consisted of art and love."]],
Never did I harm a living soul!...
Whatever misfortunes I encountered
I sought with discreet hand to succour...
Ever in pure faith,
My prayers rose
To the holy tabernacles.
Ever in pure faith,
I brought flowers to the altar.
(rising)
In the hour of sorrow,
Why, why, Lord, 
Why do you reward me thus?
I gave Jewels
For the Madonna's mantle,
And I gave my song
to the starts, to the heavens, that thus shone more beautifully.
In the hour of sorrow, 
Why, why, Lord,
Why do you reward me thus?
(sobbing)

SCARPIA
(approaching Tosca again)
Decide!

TOSCA
You want me supplicant at your feet!
(kneeling before Scarpia)
Look,
(sobbing)
I stretch out my clasped hands to you!
(raising her clasped hands)
See... look...
(in a tone of despair)
and at the mercy of a word from you,
vanquished, I await...
(dejected)

SCARPIA
You are too beautiful, Tosca, and too 
loving.
I yield. And for a paltry price;
You ask me for a life. I ask of you an instant!

TOSCA
(rising with great contempt)
Go! Go! You fill me with loathing!

(A knock at the door)

SCARPIA
Who's there?

SPOLETTA
(entering in great haste and breathless)
Excellency, Angelotti upon our arrival
killed himself.

SCARPIA
Well, then, have him hanged
Dead from the gallows! The other prisoner?

SPOLETTA
Cavalier Cavaradossi?
Everything is ready, Excellency.

TOSCA
(to herself)
God help me!

SCARPIA
(to Spoletta)
Wait.
(softly to Tosca)
Well then?
(Tosca nods; weeping with shame, she buries her face in the sofa cushions. To Spoletta)
Listen...

TOSCA
(suddenly interrupting Scarpia)
But I demand that he be freed this instant!

SCARPIA
(to Tosca)
We must pretend. I cannot
grant an open pardon. Everyone
must believe the Cavaliere is dead.
(gestures toward Spoletta)
This trusted man will see to it.

TOSCA
How can I be sure?

SCARPIA
By the orders I give him in your presence.
(to Spoletta)
Spoletta, shut the door.
(Spoletta hurriedly closes the door, then returns to Scarpia)
I have changed my mind...
The prisoner is to be shot.
(Tosca starts in terror)
Wait...
(he fixes a meaningful gaze on Spoletta, who repeatedly nods to show he understands Scarpia's intent)
Just as we did with Count Palmieri...

SPOLETTA
An execution...

SCARPIA
... A sham one! As we did
With Palmieri! 
You understand?

SPOLETTA
I understand.

SCARPIA
Go.

TOSCA
(who has been listening avidly, interjects)
I want to warn him myself.

SCARPIA
So be it.
(to Spoletta, pointing to Tosca)
Let her pass. Mind you:
at the fourth hour...
(emphasizing the point)

SPOLETTA
(deliberately)
Yes. Like Palmieri...

(Exit Spoletta. Scarpia, standing by the door, listens to Spoletta moving away; then, his expression and manner transformed, he approaches Tosca with great passion.)

SCARPIA
I kept my promise...

TOSCA
(stopping him)
Not yet.
I want a safe conduct, so that he and I
Can flee the State together.

SCARPIA
(gallantly)
So you wish to leave?

TOSCA
(with convinced tone)
Yes, for ever.

SCARPIA
Your wish shall be granted.
(goes to the writing desk; begins to write, pausing to ask Tosca:)
And which road do you prefer?

(While Scarpia writes, Tosca approaches the table and, with a trembling hand, picks up the glass of Spanish wine Scarpia had poured; but as she raises the glass to her lips, she spots a sharp, pointed knife on the table. She glances at Scarpia — who is busy writing at that moment — and, with extreme caution, tries to get hold of the knife while answering Scarpia’s questions, all the while keeping a close watch on him.)

TOSCA
The shortest!

SCARPIA
Civitavecchia?

TOSCA
Yes.
(At last she manages to take the knife, concealing it behind her back as she leans against the table, all the while keeping a close watch on Scarpia. He finishes writing the safe-conduct, affixes the seal, and folds the paper; then, opening his arms, he approaches Tosca to clasp her to him.)

SCARPIA
Tosca, now you are mine at last!
(but the voluptuous tone turns into a terrible cry — Tosca has struck him full in the chest, screaming)
Accursed woman!

TOSCA
(shouting)
This is Tosca's kiss!

SCARPIA
(in a choked voice)
Help! I’m dying!
(Scarpia stretches out his arm toward Tosca, staggering closer as if seeking help. Tosca evades him but suddenly finds herself trapped between Scarpia and the table; seeing that he is about to touch her, she pushes him away in horror. Scarpia falls.)
Help! I’m dying!

TOSCA
(with hatred toward Scarpia)
Is the blood choking you?
(Scarpia struggles in vain and tries to rise, clutching the sofa)
Killed by a woman!
You tortured me so cruelly!...
Can you still hear? Speak!... Look at me!...
I am Tosca!... Oh, Scarpia!

SCARPIA
(makes a final effort, then falls backward, suffocating)
Help, help!
(gasping)
I'm dying!

TOSCA
(leaning over Scarpia's face)
Die, you wretch! Die, die!
(Scarpia lies rigid)
He is dead! [[Now I forgive him|Dramatic shift in tone and in the music, much lower pitch in the voice]]!
(Without taking her eyes off Scarpia's corpse, she goes to the table, takes a bottle of water, and—soaking a napkin—washes her fingers; then she smooths her hair while looking in the mirror and searches for the safe-conduct on the writing desk. She remembers the safe-conduct... she looks for it on the desk but does not find it; she searches again and finally sees the safe-conduct in Scarpia's clenched hand. She lifts Scarpia's arm — letting it drop back down limply—after removing the safe-conduct, which she hides against her bosom.)
And all of Rome trembled before him!
(starts to leave but she regrets; she goes to fetch the two candles from the shelf on the left, lights them from the candelabra on the table, and then extinguishes the latter. Places one lit candle to the right of Scarpia’s head and the other to the left. Looks around again and, spotting a crucifix, takes it down from the wall; carrying it reverently, sshe kneels to place it on Scarpia’s chest. Rises and leaves with great caution, closing the door behind her.)

ACT THREE

FIRST SCENE
(The platform of Castel Sant'Angelo. On the left, a casemate: a table is placed there, holding a lamp, a large ledger, and writing materials; there is also a bench and a chair. On one wall of the casemate hangs a crucifix, with a lamp suspended before it. On the right, the opening of a small staircase leading up to the platform. In the background, the Vatican and St. Peter's. Night —  a clear sky, glittering with stars. The distant bells of a herd are heard; the sound gradually fades away.)

I take it to heart,

(The uncertain, grey light preceding dawn: church bells are ringing to announce the morning. A Jailer carrying a lantern comes up the stairs, enters the casemate, and lights the lamp hanging before the crucifix, then the one on the table. He then goes to the edge of the platform and looks down into the courtyard below to see if the squad of soldiers is arriving with the condemned man. He meets a sentry patrolling the platform; after exchanging a few words with him, he returns to the casemate, sits down, and waits, half-drowsy. Later, a squad led by a guard sergeant comes up onto the platform escorting Cavaradossi; the squad halts, and the sergeant leads Cavaradossi into the casemate, handing a paper to the Jailer. — The Jailer examines the paper, opens the register, and writes in it while questioning him:)

SECOND SCENE

JAILER
Mario Cavaradossi?
(Cavaradossi bows his head in acknowledgement. The jailer hands the pen to the Sergeant.)
For you
(The Sergeant signs the register, then leaves with the soldiers, going down the stairs)
You have one hour.
A priest awaits your call.

CAVARADOSSI
No! But one last favour
I ask of you...

JAILER
If I can...

CAVARADOSSI
I leave behind
One dear person. Allow me
To write her a few lines.
(taking a ring from his finger)
This ring is all that remains
Of my possessions!
If you will promise to give her
My last farewell,
Then it is yours...

JAILER
(hesitates a little, then accepts. He motions Cavaradossi to the chair at the table, and sits down on the bench.)
Write...

CAVARADOSSI
(remains deep in thought, then begins to write... but after tracing a few lines, he is overcome by memories and stops writing, lost in thought.)
And the stars were shining... 
And the earth was perfumed...
The gate to the garden creaked...
And a footstep grazed the sand...
Fragrant, she entered,
And fell into my arms...
Oh soft kisses, oh languid caresses,
As I trembling
Delivered her beautiful form from the veils!
Vanished forever is my dream of love,
The hour has fled,
And I die in despair!
And [[never have I loved life so much|Love here means longing, not enjoyment in the present moment.]]!
(bursts into sobs, covering his face with his hands. Spoletta enters from the stairs, accompanied by the Sergeant and followed by Tosca; the Sergeant carries a lantern. Spoletta gestures to Tosca to show him where Cavaradossi is, then summons the Jailer; he goes back down with him and the Sergeant, but not before ordering a sentry stationed at the far end to keep watch over the prisoner.)

THIRD SCENE
(Tosca, who has remained in a state of great agitation, sees Cavaradossi weeping; she rushes to his side and, unable to speak due to overwhelming emotion, lifts his head with both hands while simultaneously showing him the safe-conduct pass. Upon seeing Tosca, Cavaradossi springs to his feet in surprise and reads the document she holds out to him.)

CAVARADOSSI
(reading)
A safe-conduct for Floria Tosca...
... and for the Cavalier accompanying her.

TOSCA
(reading along with him in a breathless, agitated voice)
... and for the Cavalier accompanying her.
(to Cavaradossi with an exultant cry)
You are free!

CAVARADOSSI
(looks at the paper; spots the signature and looks pointedly at Tosca)
Scarpia!...
Scarpia yields? This is his first
Act of clemency...

TOSCA
And his last!
(picks up the safe-conduct and puts it back in a bag)

CAVARADOSSI
What are you saying?

TOSCA
(snapping)
Either your blood or my love
He demanded... my entreaties and my tears were useless.
In vain, mad with horror,
I turned to the Madonna and the Saints...
The wicked monster said: already
the gallows stretch their arms to the sky!
The drums rolled...
He laughed, the wicked monster... He laughed...
Ready to seize his prey!
"You are mine!" - Yes - I promised
Myself to his lust. There at hand
A sharp blade glittered...
He wrote the liberating paper,
And came to claim the horrible embrace...
That blade I planted in his heart.

CAVARADOSSI
You?!... by your own hand killed him? - You, pious,
You benign, - and for me!

TOSCA
My hands were 
filthy with his blood!

CAVARADOSSI
(lovingly taking her hands in his)
Oh sweet hands pure and gentle,
Oh pious hands meant for works of beauty,
for caressing children, gathering roses,
For praying, clasped, for relief from misfortune...
Then it was in you, hardened by love,
That justice placed her sacred weapons?
You dealt death, victorious hands,
Oh sweet hands pure and gentle!...

TOSCA
(disengaging her hands from his)
Listen... the hour is near. I have already. collected
(showing her bag)
my gold and jewels... A carriage is waiting...
But first... laugh at this my love... First
You will be shot - in a mock execution - with unloaded arms...
A mock punishment. Upon the shot... Fall down.
The soldiers leave... and we are safe!
And then to Civitavecchia... and there a ship...
And we're away by sea!

CAVARADOSSI
Free!

TOSCA
Who still grieves
on earth? Do you smell the scent of the roses?!...
Doesn't it seem to you that all things
are expecting the sun, filled with love?...

CAVARADOSSI
(with most tender exaltation)
Only because of you was death bitter to me,
And from you only life gains its splendor,
In my being all joy and all desire
Grow from you, as heat from a flame.
I shall see through your transfiguring eyes,
The heavens blaze and darken
And the beauty of all things remarkable
From you alone will have their voice and colour.

TOSCA
Love that found the way to save your life
Shall be our guide on earth, and on the waters
our pilot...
And will make the world gaze in wonder.
Until, united with the celestial spheres,
we shall vanish, like light clouds
high above the sea
(gazing as if in a vision)
at sunset,
(they remain stirred and silent; then Tosca, recalled by the reality of the situation, looks around uneasily)
They still don't come...
(turning to Cavaradossi with affectionate concern)
And be careful... Upon the shot
You must fall down at once...

CAVARADOSSI
(sad)
Have no fear,
I'll fall on the instant, and quite naturally.

TOSCA
(insisting)
But be careful not to hurt yourself!
With my stage knowledge,
I would know the move...

CAVARADOSSI
(interrupting and drawing her to him)
Speak to me again as you spoke before,
So sweet is the sound of your voice.

TOSCA
(surrenders herself, almost in ecstasy, then gradually becoming impassioned)
United and exultant,
we shall spread our love throughout the world,
harmonies of colors...

CAVARADOSSI
(getting excited)
We shall spread harmonies of song!

TOSCA and CAVARADOSSI
(ecstatically)
Triumphant, with new hope
the soul quivers in growing
heavenly ardour.
And in harmonious flight
The soul soars
Towards the ecstasy of love.

TOSCA
With a thousand kisses I shall seal your eyes,
And call you by a thousand names of love.

FINAL SCENE
(Meanwhile, a squad of soldiers has come up the steps, led by an officer who stations them at the back; Spoletta, the Sergeant, and the Jailer follow. Spoletta gives the necessary instructions. The sky grows brighter; it is dawn: the hour of four strikes. The Jailer approaches Cavaradossi and, removing his cap, points out the officer to him.)

JAILER
It is time!

CAVARADOSSI
I am ready.

(The Jailer takes the registry of the condemned and leaves by the stairway.)

TOSCA
(to Cavaradossi, in a very low voice and laughing under her breath)
Remember... upon the first shot... down...

CAVARADOSSI
(in a low voice, also laughing)
Down.

TOSCA
And don't get up before 
I call you...

CAVARADOSSI
No, my love!

TOSCA
And fall down properly.

CAVARADOSSI
(smiling)
Like Tosca on the stage.

TOSCA
(seeing him smile)
You mustn't laugh...

CAVARADOSSI
(serious)
Like this?

TOSCA
Like this.

(Cavaradossi follows the Officer after bidding farewell to Tosca; she positions herself on the left, inside the casemate, yet in such a way that she can spy on what is happening on the platform. She sees the Officer and the Sergeant leading Cavaradossi to the wall facing her; the Sergeant attempts to blindfold Cavaradossi, but he smilingly refuses. — These grim preparations exhaust Tosca's patience.)

TOSCA
How long is the wait!
Why are they still delaying?... The sun already rises...
Why are they still delaying?... It is only a farce,
I know... but this anguish seems to last for ever!
(l' Ufficiale e il Sergente dispongono il plotone dei soldati, impartendo gli ordini relativi)
There!... They are taking aim!...
How handsome is my Mario!
(seeing the Officer about to lower his saber, she covers her ears to avoid hearing the report; then she signals Cavaradossi with a nod to fall, saying:)
There! Die! 
(seeing him on the ground, she blows him a kiss)
There's an artist!
(The Sergeant approaches the fallen man and examines him closely; Spoletta also draws near, pushes the Sergeant aside to prevent him from delivering the coup de grâce, and then covers Cavaradossi with a cloak. The Officer lines up the soldiers; the Sergeant withdraws the sentry stationed at the far end, and then everyone — led by Spoletta — descends the stairs. Tosca is in a state of great agitation, watching these movements closely for fear that Cavaradossi, out of impatience, might move or speak before the right moment. In a hushed voice, to Cavaradossi)
Oh Mario, do not move...
They're going... Be still, they are leaving... They are going down...
(seeing the platform deserted, she goes to listen near the top of the small staircase; she stops there, trembling and breathless, as it suddenly seems to her that the soldiers, instead of moving away, are returning to the platform — she turns to Cavaradossi again, speaking in a low voice)
Not yet, don't move...
(listening —  they have all moved away; she goes to the edge and, leaning out cautiously, looks down — she runs toward Cavaradossi)
Mario, up, quickly!
Let's go!... Come on!...
(she bends down to help Cavaradossi rise; suddenly she lets out a stifled cry of terror and surprise, and looks at the hands with which she lifted the cloak)
Ah!
(she kneels, quickly removes her cloak, and springs to her feet, livid and terrified)
Dead! Dead!
(with incoherent words, sighs, and sobs, she throws herself upon Cavaradossi's body, scarcely believing the horrible fate)
Oh Mario... dead... You... Like this...
To end like this!! Like this?... Your poor Floria!
(Meanwhile, from the courtyard below the parapet and up the small staircase, the voices of Sciarrone, Spoletta, and some soldiers are heard — at first indistinct, then drawing ever closer.)

CONFUSED VOICES
Ah!...

SCIARRONE'S VOICE
Stabbed, I tell you!

CONFUSED VOICES
Scarpia?

SCIARRONE'S VOICE
Scarpia.

SPOLETTA'S VOICE
The woman is Tosca!

VARIOUS VOICES
(closer)
Don't let her escape!

THE VOICES OF SPOLETTA and SCIARRONE
(closer)
Watch
the stair exits!

(Spoletta appears on the staircase, while Sciarrone, behind him, shouts to him, pointing at Tosca:)

SCIARRONE
There she is!

SPOLETTA
(charging towards Tosca)
Ah! Tosca you will pay
For his life most dearly!

(Tosca springs to her feet and, instead of fleeing from Spoletta, violently pushes him back, replying:)

TOSCA
With my own!
(At the unexpected impact, Spoletta stumbles back; Tosca quickly breaks free, rushes past Sciarrone — who is still on the stairs — and, running to the parapet, throws herself into the void, crying out:)
Oh Scarpia, before God!
(Sciarrone and some soldiers, having scrambled up in confusion, run to the parapet and look down. Spoletta stands dumbfounded, aghast.)
